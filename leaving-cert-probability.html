Can you solve this probability challenge?

Eight independent attempts. Each has a 30% chance of success. What is the probability of exactly three successes? Pause, work it out and then check your approach.

Learn the method

Exactly three successes: the binomial approach.

1. Recognise the model

With a fixed number of independent attempts and a constant success probability, use a binomial model: X ~ B(8, 0.3). Here, n = 8, p = 0.3 and k = 3.

2. Calculate the probability

Use the probability mass formula for exactly k successes in n trials:

P(X = 3) = ⁸C₃ × 0.3³ × 0.7⁵

Since ⁸C₃ = 56, the probability is approximately 0.2541 (25.4%). The 0.7 represents the probability of failure in each attempt.

3. Check what the question actually asks

“Exactly three” is different from “at least three”. Read the wording before choosing the calculation. In an exam-style solution, show how you identified the model and substituted the values.

Make your revision stick

Try the same question again with different values of n, p or k. Explain why the combination term counts the different arrangements of success and failure. Then test whether you can solve it without looking at the formula.
